02 / THE PRINCIPLE

The Law of One Price

The same asset should trade at nearly the same price in different markets.

THE PRINCIPLE

Same gold
→ similar price

Broker A and Broker B are quoting the same underlying instrument: XAU/USD.

WHY A GAP CAN APPEAR

Different liquidity providers
Delayed price feeds
Fast market movements
Different spreads or markups
Broker systems update at different speeds
The gap is expected to close — but only if both legs can be executed.LAW OF ONE PRICE CREATES THE EXPECTATION OF CONVERGENCE

07 / THE REAL RISK

A visible gap is not automatically an arbitrage

The price difference must survive execution.

WHEN THE TRADE FAILS

The cheaper quote disappears before the buy fills
The expensive quote moves before the sell fills
One broker rejects, delays or requotes
Slippage consumes the price gap
The two contracts are not actually equivalent

THE RULE

Both legs
must execute.

Otherwise, it is a directional gold trade — not arbitrage.

Cross-broker arbitrage is a race against latency, slippage and disappearing liquidity.
